# Research brief — France school protests

**Topic ID:** france-school-protests  
**Product:** continuing thematic investigation  
**Language:** English, with sources in their original languages  
**Existing coverage:** September–22 October 2026; each new instalment must state its own verified cutoff.

## Purpose

Explain the developments, participants' demands and policy constraints through independent reporting and primary evidence. Avoid reducing a complex movement to a single political camp or treating all participants as perpetrators of violence.

## Research questions and evidence plan

| Question | Evidence needed | Competing explanation to test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What educational problems motivated mobilisation? | School-level reporting, official statistics, regional comparisons | National averages may obscure concentrated or differently defined problems. |
| Why did mobilisation expand? | Dated local reports, organisers' statements, participation changes | School grievances, policing, wider economic concerns and party involvement may contribute differently. |
| What do participants and institutions actually want? | Named statements, negotiations and independent interviews | An organisation's platform may not describe its entire constituency. |
| What happened during contested confrontations? | Incident-specific accounts, original footage provenance, official and judicial records | Competing accounts may reflect different incidents or incomplete evidence. |
| What changed after government responses? | Funded measures, implementation records and classroom-level follow-up | Announcements or redeployment may not equal additional capacity. |

## Baseline and completion criteria

Use the [version index](README.md) as the starting point, not as proof that earlier facts remain current. A new instalment should add material evidence, correct a consequential claim or substantially improve explanation. It should include supported chronology, stakeholder differences, competing interpretations, evidence limitations and a selective reading guide.

Prefer original reporting and useful depth over a fixed outlet quota. Search beyond familiar sources, including regional and fiscal coverage. Treat official and advocacy statements as evidence of their own positions unless independently corroborated.
